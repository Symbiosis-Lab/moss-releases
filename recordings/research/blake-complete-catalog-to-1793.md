# William Blake: Complete Catalog of Illuminated Works to October 1793

## Illuminated Books (Relief Etching)

### 1. All Religions are One (c. 1788)
- **Plates**: 10 (including frontispiece and title page)
- **Description**: Blake's first experiment in relief etching. Philosophical propositions arguing all religions derive from the "Poetic Genius" — the universal human imagination.
- **Key plates**: Frontispiece (figure climbing a ladder), title page (traveler)
- **Copies**: Only one complete copy survives (Copy A, Huntington Library)
- **Images**: William Blake Archive; some plates on Wikimedia Commons

### 2. There is No Natural Religion [a] and [b] (c. 1788)
- **Plates**: Series [a] ~6 plates, Series [b] ~12 plates (no single complete copy survives)
- **Description**: Two companion tracts. [a] presents Lockean empiricism; [b] refutes it — "He who sees the Infinite in all things sees God."
- **Key plates**: "Application" plate ("He who sees the Infinite..."), concluding "Therefore God becomes as we are, that we may be as he is"
- **Images**: Blake Archive, Huntington Library, Wikimedia Commons

### 3. Songs of Innocence (1789)
- **Plates**: 31 (Blake varied contents between copies)
- **Description**: First fully realized illuminated book. Pastoral lyrics from a child's perspective. Each page integrates hand-colored images with engraved text.
- **Key plates**:
  - Frontispiece (piper beneath tree, child on cloud)
  - Title page (nurse with children under tree)
  - "The Lamb"
  - "The Ecchoing Green" (2 plates)
  - "Infant Joy" (flower interior with mother and baby)
  - "The Divine Image"
  - "Holy Thursday" (children marching into St. Paul's)
  - "The Little Black Boy" (2 plates)
  - "Spring" (2 plates)
  - "The Chimney Sweeper"
  - "Night" (2 plates, lions and angels)
  - Also: The Shepherd, The Blossom, The Little Boy Lost, The Little Boy Found, Laughing Song, A Cradle Song (2 plates), Nurse's Song, A Dream, On Another's Sorrow
- **Images**:
  - Met Museum Open Access (CC0): Copy i (posthumous, uncolored)
  - Yale Center for British Art: Copy U
  - Blake Archive: Copies B, E, L, U, Z, AA
  - Fitzwilliam Museum: Copy A (1789)
  - Library of Congress: Copy Z
  - Wikimedia Commons: extensive coverage

### 4. The Book of Thel (1789)
- **Plates**: 8 (including illustrated title page)
- **Description**: Thel, a virgin spirit, questions existence by conversing with a Lily, a Cloud, a Worm, and a Clod of Clay. She approaches the gates of death and flees. Blake's gentlest myth.
- **Key plates**:
  - Title page — Thel watching figures emerge from flowers (one of Blake's most beautiful designs)
  - Plate 6 — Thel bending over the Clod of Clay and infant Worm
  - Thel's Motto — "Does the Eagle know what is in the pit?"
- **Images**: Blake Archive (copies H, I, J, O, R); Met Museum; Wikimedia Commons

### 5. The Marriage of Heaven and Hell (c. 1790-1793)
- **Plates**: 27 (Blake varied order)
- **Description**: Blake's most audacious work. Satire on Swedenborg, inversion of Paradise Lost, philosophical manifesto. Contains the Proverbs of Hell, Memorable Fancies, and the printing-house-in-hell passage describing his own method.
- **Key plates**:
  - Plate 1 (title page) — figures wrestling in flames
  - Plates 7-10 (Proverbs of Hell) — dense aphoristic text
  - Plate 15 (A Memorable Fancy) — printing house in Hell, six chambers
  - Plate 16 — the Giants who formed the world
  - Plate 20 — the angel and the abyss (Leviathan vision)
  - Plate 21 — the devil's party, monkey house
  - Plates 25-27 (A Song of Liberty) — concluding revolutionary hymn
- **Images**:
  - Met Museum (CC0): individual plates
  - Blake Archive: Copies B, C, D, E, F, G, H, I
  - Morgan Library: Copy H
  - Fitzwilliam Museum: Copy D
  - Wikimedia Commons: extensive plate-by-plate coverage

### 6. Visions of the Daughters of Albion (1793)
- **Plates**: 11
- **Description**: Oothoon, raped by Bromion and rejected by Theotormon, delivers passionate speeches against sexual repression, slavery, and possessive love. Blake's most feminist and abolitionist work.
- **Key plates**:
  - Frontispiece — three figures in bondage/despair (among Blake's most powerful images)
  - Title page — fleeing figures over waves
  - Plate 4 — Oothoon's speech, eagle tearing at her flesh (Prometheus imagery)
- **Images**: Blake Archive (copies A, C, E, F, G, J); British Museum; Wikimedia Commons

### 7. America a Prophecy (1793)
- **Plates**: 18 (including 2 full-page designs)
- **Description**: Mythologized American Revolution. Orc (revolutionary energy) confronts Urizen (tyranny). Washington, Franklin, and Paine appear alongside mythic figures. Visually the most spectacular of the Lambeth books.
- **Key plates**:
  - Frontispiece (Plate 1) — chained Orc crouching in rock
  - Plate 3 (Preludium) — Orc's liberation
  - Plate 8 — "The morning comes, the night decays" — Orc's revolutionary speech
  - Plate 12 — figures writhing in flames (one of Blake's most reproduced images)
- **Images**: Blake Archive (copies A, B, E, K, M, O, R); Morgan Library (Copy M); Yale (Copy O); Wikimedia Commons

### 8. For Children: The Gates of Paradise (1793)
- **Plates**: 18 (frontispiece, title page, 16 emblem plates with mottoes)
- **Description**: An emblem book showing the cycle of human life from birth to death. Tiny format, enigmatic.
- **Key plates**:
  - "I found him beneath a tree" (baby found under tree)
  - "Water" (figure emerging from water/egg)
  - "Aged Ignorance" clipping the wings of youth
  - "I have said to the Worm: Thou art my mother" (death)
- **Images**: Blake Archive (copies A, B, D); British Museum; Wikimedia Commons

## Image Sourcing Priority

1. **Met Museum Open Access (CC0)** — legally cleanest for reuse

3. **Yale Center for British Art** — Open Access, Paul Mellon Collection
4. **Library of Congress** — no known restrictions
5. **William Blake Archive** — scholarly standard, comprehensive
