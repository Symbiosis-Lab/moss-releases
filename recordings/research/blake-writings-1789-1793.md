# William Blake: Surviving Writings 1789-1793

## The Rossetti Manuscript / Notebook (c. 1790-1793)

Blake used a sketchbook (now British Library, Add MS 49460) that his brother Robert had partly used before his death in 1787. Blake filled it from both ends — poems from one end, sketches and notes from the other. Dante Gabriel Rossetti later owned it.

### Draft Poems That Became Songs of Experience

Many revised before engraving. Key examples of the revision process:

- **"The Tyger"** — multiple drafts showing intensive revision. Whole stanzas cancelled. "Could," "Dare," "grasp," "clasp" crossed out and reworked. More heavily revised than any other poem.
- **"London"** — draft originally read "dirty street" and "dirty Thames" before Blake changed both to "charter'd" — adding political weight.
- **"A Poison Tree"** — originally titled "Christian Forbearance"
- Also: The Sick Rose, The Clod & the Pebble, The Garden of Love, Infant Sorrow (longer draft version), The Fly, The Angel, The Human Abstract

### Poems NOT Included in the Illuminated Books

Raw, personal, unpublished. From c.1790-93:

- "Never seek to tell thy Love" ("Never pain to tell thy love")
- "I laid me down upon a bank"
- "I saw a chapel all of gold"
- "I asked a thief to steal me a peach"
- "I heard an Angel singing"
- "A cradle song" (darker than the Innocence version)
- "How to know Love from Deceit"
- "Motto to the Songs of Innocence & of Experience"
- "An ancient Proverb" ("Remove away that blackning church...")
- "Let the Brothels of Paris be opened"
- "Who will exchange his own fire side..."
- "Fayette" fragments
- "Soft Snow"
- "An Answer to the Parson"
- "Merlin's prophecy"
- "Day" / "The sword sung on the barren heath"
- Various epigrams and satirical verses (~63 politically charged poems from c.1793)

### The Stark Entry (June 1793)

"I say I shan't live five years. And if I live one it will be a Wonder."

## Annotations on Other Authors' Books

### Annotations to Lavater's *Aphorisms on Man* (c. 1788-1789)

Blake annotated his copy of J.C. Lavater's *Aphorisms on Man* (translated by Henry Fuseli, 1788). Extensive marginal notes — some of Blake's most quotable early prose.

Key annotations:
- "The most sublime act is to set another before you"
- Responses to ~200 of Lavater's aphorisms
- Marked aphorisms with X, !, or checkmarks alongside prose comments
- Back flyleaf: extensive notes on religion and character

Source: Huntington Library (annotated copy). Full text in Erdman's *Complete Poetry & Prose of William Blake*.

### Annotations to Swedenborg's *Divine Love and Divine Wisdom* (c. 1789)

Blake and Catherine attended the first general conference of the New Jerusalem Church (Swedenborgian) in April 1789 and signed the register. Blake annotated his copy.

- Initially sympathetic, then increasingly critical
- Challenges to Swedenborg's dualism and predestination
- These annotations feed directly into The Marriage of Heaven and Hell's satirical attack

Source: British Museum. Erdman's edition.

### Annotations to Swedenborg's *Divine Providence* (c. 1790)

More hostile than the *Divine Love* annotations.
- "Priesthood" and "Predestination" rejected forcefully
- "Lies & Priestcraft" written beside certain passages

**Note**: Annotations on Reynolds' *Discourses* are c.1808, NOT from this period.

## Letters

Very few survive from 1789-1793:

### To Willey Reveley (October 18, 1791)
The only confirmed surviving letter from this period. Blake responds to a request (probably from the Wollstonecraft/Johnson circle) about engraving work. Brief, businesslike.

### Lost Letters
- Letters to John Flaxman from this period are lost (earliest surviving: September 1800)
- Letters to George Cumberland from this period are lost (earliest surviving: April 6, 1795)

## The 1793 Prospectus: "To the Public"

Single-leaf broadside, relief etched. Dated October 10, 1793. Blake's declaration of independence as a publisher.

Full text:

> October 10, 1793.
>
> TO THE PUBLIC.
>
> The Labours of the Artist, the Poet, the Musician, have been proverbially attended by poverty and obscurity; this was never the fault of the Public, but was owing to a neglect of means to propagate such works as have wholly absorbed the Man of Genius. Even Milton and Shakespeare could not publish their own works.
>
> This difficulty has been obviated by the Author of the following productions now presented to the Public; who has invented a method of Printing both Letter-press and Engraving in a style more ornamental, uniform, and grand, than any before discovered, while it produces works at less than one fourth of the expense.
>
> [Works listed with prices:]
> 1. Job, a Historical Engraving — 12s.
> 2. Edward and Elinor, a Historical Engraving — 10s. 6d.
> 3. America, a Prophecy, in Illuminated Printing — 10s. 6d.
> 4. Visions of the Daughters of Albion, in Illuminated Printing — 7s. 6d.
> 5. The Book of Thel — 3s.
> 6. The Marriage of Heaven and Hell — 7s. 6d.
> 7. Songs of Innocence — 5s.
> 8. Songs of Experience — 5s.
> 9. The History of England, a small book of Engravings — 3s.
> 10. The Gates of Paradise, a small book of Engravings — 3s.
>
> The Illuminated Books are Printed in Colours, and on the most beautiful wove paper that could be procured.
>
> No Subscriptions for the numerous great works now in hand are asked, for none are wanted; but the Author will produce his works, and offer them to sale at a fair price.

## Prose Within the Illuminated Books

### The Marriage of Heaven and Hell (c. 1790-93)

Contains Blake's most extensive prose:

- **"The Voice of the Devil"** — prose manifesto rejecting body/soul dualism
- **Milton being "of the Devil's party without knowing it"** — Blake's reading of Paradise Lost
- **Proverbs of Hell** (70 proverbs) — including:
  - "The road of excess leads to the palace of wisdom"
  - "The tygers of wrath are wiser than the horses of instruction"
  - "Eternity is in love with the productions of time"
  - "No bird soars too high, if he soars with his own wings"
  - "A fool sees not the same tree that a wise man sees"
- **A Memorable Fancy #1** — walking among fires of Hell, collecting proverbs
- **A Memorable Fancy #2** — the printing house in Hell (six chambers of creation — Blake's allegory for his own method)
- **A Memorable Fancy #3** — dinner with Isaiah and Ezekiel (Blake's theory of prophecy)
- **A Memorable Fancy #4** — the angel and the abyss (Leviathan, monkey house, church vault)
- **A Memorable Fancy #5** — the angel converted to a devil
- **"A Song of Liberty"** — concluding prose-poem: "One Law for the Lion & Ox is Oppression"
