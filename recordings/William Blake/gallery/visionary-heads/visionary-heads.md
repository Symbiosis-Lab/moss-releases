Mr John Varley the astrologer, an old friend of Linnell's, would have me sit up with him of an evening in his rooms at No. 10 Great Titchfield Street. He was persuaded I could call up Visions to order — kings, generals, ancient Britons, the spirits of the dead — & he set himself to record the Heads as I drew them.

It became a game. Mr Varley would name a Spirit; I would say *He is just come up the stairs*; I would draw the Head. Then I would say *He is sitting for me now*. Mr Varley took it for serious natural Philosophy. I took it for play that was not less than serious — for I did see the Heads, in the way that I had seen the Angels in the Tree at Peckham Rye when I was eight. *The vision is a thing seen, not a thing imagined.*

The most famous of these Heads is the **Ghost of a Flea**. A spirit told me that fleas are inhabited by the souls of bloodthirsty men, & I drew the figure as it stood before me: a great man-shaped Reptile, scaled & green, carrying a Cup of Blood. Mr Varley said: *Take care, Mr Blake, the Flea is in this room*. I said: *Now he is in my picture*. I drew him twice, in pencil & in tempera; the tempera Mr Tatham gave to Mr Linnell, & it sits at Tate now.

Heads I drew that survive:

- The Ghost of a Flea (pencil + tempera, Tate)
- William Wallace
- King Edward the First
- The Man Who Built the Pyramids
- The Man Who Instructed Mr Blake in Painting in his Dreams
- Cancer the Crab (zodiacal)
- King Saul
- King Solomon
- Caractacus
- Owen Glendower
- Boadicea
- Lais of Corinth
- The Empress Maud
- *Many others; a sketchbook of them passed to Tatham at my death.*
