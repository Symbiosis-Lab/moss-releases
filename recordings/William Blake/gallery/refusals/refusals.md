A small room. The Refusals.

A Reader who sees only what I made will not understand what I would not make. Here are four:

---

## I would not engrave after Stothard for Cromek.

In the year six, Mr Cromek had me design twenty plates for Blair's *Grave*. He then took the Engraving away from me & gave it to Schiavonetti. I went on with the Designs but I did not pretend it was no Theft.

In the same year, Cromek went to Mr Stothard (who had been my friend for thirty years) & commissioned him to paint my *Canterbury Pilgrims* over again. Stothard did not refuse, & had it engraved by Schiavonetti also. *I would not have done the same to him.*

I refused thereafter to take any commission Cromek offer'd, & I engraved my own *Canterbury Pilgrims* myself, & sold it by Subscription. There were not many Subscribers. There were enough.

---

## I would not paint Mr Hayley's polite watercolours of ladies.

At Felpham Mr Hayley set me to engrave miniature heads of his friends, & to paint polite watercolours after his preferred Painters. He call'd this *taming* me. He thought my Visions were a kind of nervous Sickness.

I did what he asked for three years & I starved my own Work. By the third year I knew I must leave. The Spectre was on me, the Spectrous Fiend that Hayley had unwittingly fed.

In the letter to Mr Butts of the sixth of July, in the year three, I wrote it out plainly: *I am determined to be no longer Pestered with his Genteel Ignorance.*

I did not refuse to engrave for Hayley — that paid the rent. I refused to *become* him.

---

## I would not exhibit at the Royal Academy at last.

I exhibited at the Royal Academy six times between the year eighty & the year eight, & then I would no more. The Refusal was not theirs against me but ripened mutually over thirty years. The terms had become impossible.

The Academy will hang Oil-paintings & not Fresco — that is, not Water-colour, which is the antient method I have restored. They asked me to switch to Oil. I would not. So in the year nine I held my own Exhibition at my Brother's house in Broad Street, with a *Descriptive Catalogue* of my own composition, that the Public might judge the Work without the Academy's filter.

Mr Robert Hunt of the *Examiner* called me an unfortunate lunatic. The Public did not come.

I did not regret the Exhibition. The Academy's terms were not mine.

---

## I would not write what the Knaves wanted.

I had many Patrons who offer'd Commission for tame work — Mr Trusler the Doctor of Divinity who wanted Moral Designs without Vision; Mr Cromek who wanted illustrations to *please*; the Royal Academy's painters who wanted me to *generalise*.

To Generalise is to be an Idiot. To Particularise is the Alone Distinction of Merit.

I would not generalise. I would not produce, even for bread, the work that betrays the Imagination.

---
