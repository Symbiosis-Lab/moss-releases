I painted in Fresco, not in Oil. By Fresco I mean Water-Colour on a smoothly-prepared ground, with carpenter's glue as binder — the antient method of the great Italians, which I have restored. The Royal Academy will not hang Fresco; they will hang Oil only. This is one of my Refusals, & one of theirs.

The temperas have suffered with time more than the watercolours — the glue cracks, the paint flakes — but many survive. They were sold mostly to Mr Butts in my middle years, & some to Mr Hayley for his library at Felpham, & later some to Mr Linnell.

Notable temperas:

- **Heads of the Poets** (~18 works, 1800–01) — painted for Mr Hayley's library at Felpham, hung over the bookcases. Chaucer, Spenser, Shakespeare, Milton, Dante, Homer, Camoens, Otway, Cowper, Klopstock, & others. *(Manchester City Galleries.)*
- **Bathsheba at the Bath** *(Tate.)*
- **Adam Naming the Beasts** *(Glasgow Museums, Kelvingrove.)*
- **Eve Naming the Birds** *(Glasgow Museums.)*
- **Christ in the Sepulchre, Guarded by Angels** *(Victoria & Albert.)*
- **The Spiritual Form of Pitt Guiding Behemoth** *(Tate. Shown at the 1809 Exhibition as Number II.)*
- **The Spiritual Form of Nelson Guiding Leviathan** *(Tate. Shown at the 1809 Exhibition as Number I.)*
- **Satan Calling Up His Legions** *(Victoria & Albert.)*
- **The Ghost of a Flea** *(Tate. Drawn during the Varley sessions — see Visionary Heads.)*
