In the year ninety-five at Hercules Buildings I made twelve large prints in color, by a method that was mine alone. I painted the design in opaque colors upon a smooth millboard, & before the paint dried I laid a sheet of paper on it & pulled an impression. Each pull is unique — the colors come away differently, no two prints alike. The method gave a strange thick texture, between fresco & monotype, which I have never seen in any other work.

The twelve subjects:

1. **Newton** — Newton with his compasses, blind in the deep, the dragon-eel beneath him. *(Tate, via Wikimedia Commons — Tate's own terms forbid web reuse, so the public-domain print comes from elsewhere.)*
2. **Nebuchadnezzar** — the king as beast, crawling on all fours, naked in the wilderness. *(Tate / Wikimedia)*
3. **Pity** — *and pity, like a naked new-born babe, striding the blast*. From Macbeth. *(Tate / Wikimedia)*
4. **Hecate, or The Night of Enitharmon's Joy** — the threefold goddess in the moonlight. *(Tate / Wikimedia)*
5. **Elohim Creating Adam** — Adam half-formed, the worm at his side. *(Tate / Wikimedia)*
6. **Satan Exulting over Eve** — Satan triumphant above the sleeping Eve. *(Getty Museum, J. Paul Getty 84.GG.39, open content)*
7. **God Judging Adam** — the seated God reading the law to Adam. *(Tate / Wikimedia. Formerly mis-titled "Elijah in the Fiery Chariot" until Martin Butlin re-identified it in 1965.)*
8. **Lamech and His Two Wives** — Lamech grieving over the boy he has killed in error. *(Tate / Wikimedia)*
9. **Naomi entreating Ruth and Orpah** — the three women parting. *(Victoria & Albert Museum)*
10. **Christ Appearing to the Apostles** — the risen Christ to the eleven. *(National Gallery of Art, Washington — open access)*
11. **The House of Death** — Death over the plague-stricken. *(Tate / Wikimedia)*
12. **The Good and Evil Angels** — the two figures contending for the child. *(Tate / Wikimedia)*

I sold most of these to Mr Butts, who has them yet. Some went to other hands; they are dispersed across the museums of Britain & America. No two impressions are alike, & no two prints are quite the same — the method made each its own work.
