This file is a marked placeholder. Ellis & Yeats's 1893 edition of *Vala / The Four Zoas* prints clearly-headed textual apparatus (collation of Blake's manuscript emendations) at vol. III pp. 174ff, but the poem body runs continuously across many pages without per-Night section breaks, making line-level extraction unreliable from OCR alone.

- David V. Erdman, ed., *The Complete Poetry and Prose of William Blake* (Anchor, rev. 1988), pp. 386–407. *In copyright.*

## Ellis & Yeats's textual apparatus to Night IX, vol. III, pp. 174ff

**NIGHT THE NINTH.**

The heading of this Night suggests that it was conceived at the time when the title of the poem was to have been *The Death and Judgment of the Ancient Man*. The name "Albion" is not used in the MS. here.

In this Night, line 32 is later, also line 50. Lines 113 and 127 are later. They seem utterances produced by the realization of the subject of the poem in the mind of the poet as he sat reading his own manuscript. The same may be said of lines 139 and 140, also insertions.

The lines 200 to 202 are also insertions. In the next line it is noteworthy that *The Eternal Man* is the phrase employed. "Albion" is not used after Night IV.

Line 256 is later, and also lines 354 to 256 [sic].

Lines 328 and 329, later; also lines 378, 379 and 383.

Lines 487 to 490 are also later. Line 584 is later, also line 617, repeated from an earlier page.

No other changes of the MS., other than the corrections of separate words given in the notes on the texts above, have been found in Blake's hand as contemporary with the writing itself.

*— End of Ellis & Yeats textual apparatus.*
