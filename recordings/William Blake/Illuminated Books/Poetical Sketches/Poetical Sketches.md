These verses are the work of my youth — written between my twelfth & my twentieth year, before I had found the Method.

Mr Flaxman & Mr Mathew had them printed in letterpress at their own expense in the year eighty-three, when I was twenty-six. There were faults in the production — the type small, the paper indifferent — & I never put it out to general sale. I gave copies to a few friends, & kept the rest.

It is the only Letterpress I ever consented to.

Contained herein: *To Spring*, *To Summer*, *To Autumn*, *To Winter*, *To the Evening Star*, *To Morning*, *Fair Elenor*, four Songs (*How sweet I roam'd*, *My silks and fine array*, *Love and harmony combine*, *I love the jocund dance*), *Mad Song*, *To the Muses*, *Gwin King of Norway*, *An Imitation of Spenser*, *Blind Man's Buff*, *King Edward the Third* (a dramatic fragment), *Prologue to King John*, *A War Song to Englishmen*, *The Couch of Death*, *Contemplation*, *Samson*.

> Whether on Idas shady brow,
> Or in the chambers of the East,
> The chambers of the sun, that now
> From antient melody have ceas'd...
>
> How have you left the antient love
> That bards of old enjoy'd in you!
> The languid strings do scarcely move!
> The sound is forc'd, the notes are few!
>
> *— To the Muses*
