William Blake/Journal

lambeth

Blake and Catherine moved from 28 Poland Street, Soho, to No. 13 Hercules Buildings, Lambeth, in late 1790. They lived there approximately ten years, until the move to Felpham in September 1800. The house was a three-storey terrace with a small back garden. The rent is not given in the principal biographies; later figures vary — Mona Wilson (1927) gives £40 p.a., the Lambeth Manor rate-books for the period (cited in Bentley, Blake Records 2nd ed. 2004, p. 64) show rents nearer £18–22 p.a. for the Hercules Buildings row.

Gilchrist records one anecdote of the Lambeth years, transmitted to him via Thomas Butts:

At the end of the little garden in Hercules Buildings there was a summer-house. Mr. Butts calling one day found Mr. and Mrs. Blake sitting in this summer-house, freed from 'those troublesome disguises' which have prevailed since the Fall. 'Come in!' cried Blake; 'its only Adam and Eve, you know!' Husband and wife had been reciting passages from Paradise Lost, in character, and the garden of Hercules Buildings had to represent the Garden of Eden.

The anecdote has been disputed since the first printing. Gilchrist himself notes that Linnell "regards it with incredulity." A stronger objection is that Butts denied the story directly to Linnell — John Story, Life of John Linnell (1892) vol. I p. 244 records this exchange. Ellis & Yeats (1893 vol. I p. 42) further argued that Blake was theologically opposed to Paradise Lost at this period (the Marriage of Heaven and Hell dates to c. 1790) and "never heard to recite Milton at any other time." Most subsequent biographers (Symons 1907, Wilson 1927, Bentley 2001, Ackroyd 1995) have nevertheless accepted the anecdote as substantially true, though embellished.

The illuminated books made at Hercules Buildings include Songs of Innocence and of Experience (1789, completed 1794), The Book of Thel (1789), The Marriage of Heaven and Hell (c. 1790), Visions of the Daughters of Albion (1793), America (1793), Europe (1794), The First Book of Urizen (1794), and the Lambeth continental prophecies (1795).

Source: Gilchrist 1880, vol. I, p. 112 (the summer-house passage); Story 1892, vol. I, p. 244 (Linnell on Butts's denial); Bentley Blake Records 2nd ed. 2004 p. 64 (rate-book rent).
