William Blake

Letters

"Readers who only know the illuminated books do not know me until they know the letters." (Per Blake's pass-10 review of this archive.)

The Felpham years are the Butts cycle — Blake writing from his cottage by the sea to the patron who paid him when no one else would. The deathbed years are the Linnell cycle — letters from South Molton Street and Fountain Court to the young friend who fed his last winters. Hayley sits between, the patron Blake later said had drained him, but the trial-cluster letters of 1804 — added in pass-13 — show the man at his most defended and most grateful, writing immediately after returning to London from the Chichester court. Cumberland carries the early relief-etching manifesto (1795) and the deathbed letter (April 1827). Trusler receives the great defence — "I see Every thing I paint In This World."

The letters are where the engraver stops being a figure on a plate and becomes a man writing about the sea, his wife's health, his brother's grave, and the painters he cannot persuade.

By cycle

Butts (9 letters, 1800–1803). The Felpham correspondence — verse-epistles, the fourfold vision, the Riposo painting, and Blake's own account of the Scofield soldier incident that brought him to the sedition trial.

  • 1800-09-23 - To Butts — Arrival at Felpham
  • 1800-10-02 - To Butts — thanks for Butts' answering verses
  • 1801-05-10 - To Butts — on the discipline of patron-work
  • 1801-09-11 - To Butts — plans for another Butts portrait
  • 1802-01-10 - To Butts — long apology after silence
  • 1802-11-22 - To Butts — verses: "Now I a fourfold vision see"
  • 1803-04-25 - To Butts — sending the Riposo
  • 1803-07-06 - To Butts — the Riposo painting
  • 1803-08-16 - To Butts Scofield — the soldier incident in Blake's own words

Linnell (8 letters, 1819–1827). The deathbed cycle — gratitude for the £10 instalments, dandelion prescriptions, the relapse after the Hampstead visit, the final letter six weeks before death.

  • 1819-10-04 - To Linnell First — earliest extant Linnell letter (the patron Blake would die under)
  • 1825-10-11 - To Linnell
  • 1825-11-10 - To Linnell — first explicit Job-plates reference in the Linnell correspondence
  • 1826-02-01 - To Linnell — too ill, mutton chop instead
  • 1826-07-29 - To Linnell — Dr Young's dandelion prescription
  • 1826-08-01 - To Linnell — mid-illness summer letter
  • 1827-04-25 - To Linnell — recovery in progress
  • 1827-07-03 - To Linnell — relapse; the last Linnell letter

Hayley (13 letters, 1800–1805). From Felpham, then after the return to South Molton Street. The 1804 cluster (added pass-13 + 14) is the trial sequence — Blake writing from London immediately after his acquittal at Chichester for the Scofield charge.

  • 1800-11-26 - To Hayley Felpham Arrival — Milton, Homer, Camoens, Ercilla, Ariosto, Spenser
  • 1803-10-26 - To Hayley
  • 1804-01-14 - To Hayley Post Trial — "Flaxman welcomed me with kind affection and generous exultation in my escape from the arrows of darkness" — London the day after the trial
  • 1804-04-27 - To Hayley — trial aftermath; the Romney biography work
  • 1804-05-04 - To Hayley — on Romney's portraits and the engraving plan
  • 1804-05-11 - To Hayley — Hayley's reading list and the Romney engravings
  • 1804-06-22 - To Hayley — three sublime Romney designs in Blake's lodgings
  • 1804-09-10 - To Hayley — returning books; the Romney project continues
  • 1804-10-23 - To Hayley Truchsess — "I was again enlightened with the light I enjoyed in my youth"
  • 1804-12-13 - To Hayley — sending proofs of The Shipwreck and The Portrait engravings, with Flaxman's approval
  • 1804-12-28 - To Hayley Rose Died — on Samuel Rose's death
  • 1805-01-22 - To Hayley — closes the trial-aftermath year; Phillips' offer for the Ballads
  • 1805-12-11 - To Hayley — on the reception of Blair's Grave

Cumberland (6 letters, 1795–1827). The earliest extant Blake letter (the relief-etching manifesto) through the deathbed letter twelve days before death.

  • 1795-12-06 - To Cumberland — Russell's earliest entry; relief-etching method
  • 1796-12-23 - To Cumberland
  • 1799-08-26 - To Cumberland
  • 1808-12-19 - To Cumberland
  • 1827-04-02 - To Cumberland Final
  • 1827-04-12 - To Cumberland Final — the deathbed letter: "the real man, the imagination, which liveth for ever"

Other (5 letters).

  • 1791-10-18 - To Reveley — earliest letter (engraving question)
  • 1799-08-23 - To Trusler — manifesto: "I see Every thing I paint In This World"
  • 1800-09-12 - To Flaxman — verse-letter to Flaxman
  • 1801-10-19 - To Flaxman
  • 1818-06-09 - To Dawson Turner — Blake's own price-list of the illuminated books
