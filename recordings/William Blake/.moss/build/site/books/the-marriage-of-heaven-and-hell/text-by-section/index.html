William Blake/Books/The Marriage of Heaven and Hell

Text by Section

"Songs gave the reader the lyrics; the Marriage gives him the manifesto." (Per Blake's pass-13 review of this archive.)

Nine named sections, in the order Blake engraved them, with plate ranges from standard Blake scholarship:

  1. The Argument (Plate 2) — Rintrah roars; the just man in the wilderness
  2. The Voice of the Devil (Plates 3–6) — the manifesto: errors of the Bibles, contraries, the Note on Milton
  3. A Memorable Fancy: Walking Among the Fires of Hell (Plates 6–7) — the Proverbs are gathered
  4. Proverbs of Hell (Plates 7–10) — "The road of excess leads to the palace of wisdom"
  5. A Memorable Fancy: Isaiah and Ezekiel Dined With Me (Plates 12–13) — Blake interrogates the Hebrew prophets
  6. A Memorable Fancy: I Was in a Printing-house in Hell (Plate 15) — the six chambers of relief-etching
  7. A Memorable Fancy: An Angel Came to Me (Plates 17–20) — the Angel's prepared hell becomes a moonlit bank
  8. A Memorable Fancy: A Devil in a Flame of Fire (Plates 22–24) — the Devil's gospel: "No virtue can exist without breaking these ten commandments"
  9. A Song of Liberty (Plates 25–27) — "For every thing that lives is Holy" — the closing chorus

The plate-text on the engraved pages differs in a few minor ways from this reading text (copy-state capitalisation, plate 3's prose-headnote presence varies by copy). For exact plate-state reading, the facsimile copies in ../copy-f-morgan/ and ../copy-c-bound/ are authoritative.
