These eight or ten years I have lived in South Molton Street & worked, & few have come.

The 1809 Exhibition closed without selling a picture. The Catalogue I distributed to those who came in; one or two of them mocked me in the press. Mr Hunt of the *Examiner* called me an unfortunate lunatic. I have not forgiven him; I have not lost time replying.

I have engraved the *Canterbury Pilgrims* — my own design, in a great plate, sold by subscription with my own Prospectus. The Subscribers are few but they are true. Mr Butts, Mr Cumberland, Mr Linnell (who is a new friend, since the year fifteen) — these are not Cromek & Stothard.

In these silent years I have written into the Notebook: the *Public Address* on the question of Engraving & Painting; the *Vision of the Last Judgment*; the long poem the *Everlasting Gospel*; & many epigrams against the Knaves & Fools of the Schools.

I shall not put these on copper. They are for whoever finds them after me.

(Catherine has been well, then ill, then well again. We eat plainly. The press still works.)
