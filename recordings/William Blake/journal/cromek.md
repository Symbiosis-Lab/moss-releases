title: Cromek & Stothard

Mr Robert Cromek, a Yorkshire Engraver turn'd Publisher, came to me at South Molton Street in the year five & commission'd designs for Blair's *Grave*. He paid me twenty guineas for twenty drawings, with the understanding that I should engrave them myself.

He then took the engraving away from me & gave it to Mr Schiavonetti. I was not consulted, nor paid for the engraving I did not do.

The same Cromek, having seen my tempera of Chaucer's Canterbury Pilgrims in my parlour, went directly to my old friend Mr Stothard & commission'd him to paint the same subject. Mr Stothard, who knows me these thirty years, did not refuse. He paint'd a *Canterbury Pilgrims* of his own, exhibited it, & had it engraved by Mr Schiavonetti.

The world calls Stothard's the original. The world is wrong. Mine was painted first; mine is the true picture; & I will engrave it myself this year, in a great folio plate, & sell it by Subscription, & the Public may judge between us.

I have written all this out at length in a Public Address which I shall print one day. For now I keep it in the Notebook & wait.

*Hired Knaves wear Fetters whom they hope to enslave others, by laying them on themselves first.*
