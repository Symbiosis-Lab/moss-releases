The press is a Star-Wheel rolling press, of iron, set on a stout oak frame. I bought it second-hand & it has serv'd me well.

The plates are of copper, about the thickness of a shilling, hammered flat & polished to a glass. I draw upon them in reverse, with a brush dipt in a ground compleat of asphaltum dissolved in oil of turpentine. Every Letter, every Leaf & tendril, every Figure must be set down mirror-wise, that the printed sheet may read True.

When the drawing is dry the plate goes into a bath of aquafortis — nitric acid, diluted — & there it stays till the plain surfaces have been eaten away to the depth of a shilling's thickness. The asphaltum holds the lines & letters above the bite.

The plate is wiped, inked, & laid face-up on the press-bed. The paper is dampened by Catherine the night before & laid betwixt sheets of damp blanket. The bed runs thro' the rollers; the impression is taken.

The colouring is done afterwards, by hand, sheet by sheet. Catherine sits at the right of the table; I sit at the left. We use the same brushes.

The whole proceeds at less than one fourth of the expense of the common Engraving — which my Prospectus declares.
