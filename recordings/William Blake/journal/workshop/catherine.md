Catherine Boucher of Battersea, daughter of a market-gardener; I married her on the eighteenth of August in the year eighty-two, at St Mary's, where she signed the Book with a Mark.

I taught her to read & to write & then to colour, & she has done all three these forty-five years. The press is hers as much as mine. The ink is mixed by her. The plates are tinted by her hand on most of the copies that go out — & I have always approved.

When I worked late at the Songs she sat by me & reminded me to eat. When the soldiers came at Felpham she was steady. When I painted the great Visions she set the cups of colour in a half-circle on the table the way I like them.

She is the same Kate now that she was at twenty-two, only more so.

When I am gone she will outlive me four years & then come to me again.
