In the summer of eighty-eight, more than a year now since Robert was taken, he stood in my chamber as plainly as you stand. He laid before me the manner of the Etching, line for line, even to the stopping-out varnish.

I tried it that same week & it answer'd.

The plates are of copper. I draw upon them in reverse with a ground compleat of asphaltum dissolved in turpentine — every Letter, every Leaf, every Figure set down mirror-wise that they may print True. The plain surfaces I eat away with aquafortis until the lines & letters stand up in relief. Then the plate is inked & impressed upon the dampened sheet.

This is a method of Printing which combines the Painter & the Poet, & does the work at less than one fourth of the expense of the common Engraving. I have not seen it done by another hand.

The Songs are made by Robert's hand as well as mine.
