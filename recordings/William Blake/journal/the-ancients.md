There is a circle of young men who call themselves *the Ancients*. They come to Fountain Court of an evening — Samuel Palmer the eldest tho' he is but nineteen, George Richmond, Edward Calvert, Frederick Tatham, & sometimes John Linnell who is older but counts as one of them.

They sit on the floor & on the bed & in the one good chair. Catherine makes tea, & sometimes a plain cake. They ask me to recite, & I do — passages from *Milton*, sometimes a Song, sometimes the Address to the Christians from *Jerusalem*.

Palmer is the one I love best. He has the gift of vision in him already — he sees pastoral England the way I see Eternity, & it is the same Country only nearer in.

Richmond made my portrait when I was sick this spring. Calvert is a sailor turn'd Engraver & makes small pastoral cuts of woodland that have the smell of leaf-mould in them.

They call me *the Interpreter*. I do not refuse the title.

These are my Sons that I did not have of Catherine's body, & they will carry on after me. There is a Church coming of which I am the first Member.
