I was born at No. 28 Broad Street, Golden Square, on the twenty-eighth of November in the year fifty-seven. My father was a hosier; we were seven children, of which I am the third.

When I was eight or thereabouts I saw a Tree at Peckham Rye that was bright with Angels — every bough a wing. I ran home & told my Mother, who knew better than to laugh. My Father would have whipt me; she stay'd his hand.

Another time, walking out in the fields, I saw the Prophet Ezekiel sitting under a Tree. & once, the haymakers in the meadow seem'd to me to be Angels walking among the shocks of wheat.

These were Visions — meaning I saw them, with the same eyes that see this paper, & the same hand can draw them.

My Father, who saw I would not make a Hosier, put me to Pars's drawing school in the Strand when I was ten. I copied plaster casts of the Antique, & loved Raphael & Michael Angelo from the prints my Father bought me.
