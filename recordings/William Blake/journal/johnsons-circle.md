Joseph Johnson opened his bookshop at 72 St Paul's Churchyard, London, in 1761 and rapidly became the principal commercial patron of dissenting and radical writers in late-Georgian England. Knowles records Sir Andrew Hamilton's introduction of Fuseli to Johnson c. 1764:

> The latter had but recently begun business on his own account, but he had already acquired the character which he retained during life, — that of a man of great integrity, an encourager of literary men as far as his means extended, and an excellent judge of their productions.

Blake's commercial relationship with Johnson dates from at least 1779 and ran intermittently until Johnson's death in December 1809. Johnson commissioned:

- The Lavater *Aphorisms on Man* plates (1788; Blake's translation-frontispiece engraving)
- *Original Stories from Real Life* by Mary Wollstonecraft (six engravings, 1791)
- The proof sheets of *The French Revolution: A Poem in Seven Books, Book the First* (1791 — set in type, withheld from publication)
- Stedman's *Narrative of a Five Years' Expedition against the Revolted Negroes of Surinam* (sixteen engravings, 1793–1796)
- Various plates after Fuseli, Stothard, and others

[The exact composition of Johnson's Tuesday dinners is recorded in multiple contemporary memoirs: Knowles for Fuseli's testimony; Godwin's *Memoirs of the Author of A Vindication of the Rights of Woman* (1798, public domain) for Wollstonecraft's testimony; *The Gentleman's Magazine* obituary January 1810 for the wider gathering after Johnson's death. The dinners typically convened on Tuesdays; regular attendees over the 1780s–90s included Thomas Paine, Joseph Priestley, William Godwin, Mary Wollstonecraft, Henry Fuseli, John Aikin, Anna Letitia Barbauld, and Blake intermittently.]

Johnson was prosecuted in 1798 for selling Gilbert Wakefield's *Reply to the Bishop of Llandaff* and was imprisoned in the King's Bench Prison for nine months. He continued to entertain his circle there.

*Sources:* Knowles, *Life and Writings of Henry Fuseli* (1831), vol. I; *Gentleman's Magazine*, January 1810, vol. 80 pt. 1, pp. 91–93 (Johnson obituary, public domain).
