Mr Joseph Johnson the bookseller keeps a table at No. 72, St Paul's Churchyard. Every Tuesday at three he sets out a plain dinner — a boiled fowl, a leg of mutton, rice pudding — & we sit till seven or eight & talk Politicks & Religion & the Trade.

There comes Tom Paine, who is on his second part of the *Rights of Man* this year; Dr Priestley, whose house was burnt at Birmingham last summer; Mr Godwin, who is writing a book about Justice; Mrs Wollstonecraft, who is writing one about Women; Henry Fuseli, who paints the Nightmare; & Captain Stedman, who has come back from Surinam with terrible drawings of the Slaves there, which I am engraving for him.

I engraved Mrs Wollstonecraft's *Original Stories from Real Life* this year — six plates. She is sharp-spoken & does not flatter; I respect her.

Last September, when the warrant came out for Paine, I told him plainly: *Tom — you must not go home tonight. Go to Dover & take ship.* He went. They say I exaggerate; but he went, & he is alive in Paris this hour, & Mr Pitt's officers came to his lodging & found him fled.

Mr Johnson set my *French Revolution* in type this same year & then drew back. The political climate is grown sharp.
