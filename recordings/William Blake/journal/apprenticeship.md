On 4 August 1772, William Blake was bound apprentice to James Basire, Engraver, of No. 31 Great Queen Street, Lincoln's Inn Fields. The indenture term was seven years. Blake's father paid a premium of fifty guineas (£52 10s). Blake was fourteen years old.

Both Tatham and Gilchrist record that Basire set the young Blake to drawing the monuments in Westminster Abbey as a regular task — work that Blake later credited with forming his sense of "Gothic Form."

*Source:* Frederick Tatham, *Life of William Blake* (c. 1832; via Russell ed., 1906); Alexander Gilchrist, *Life of William Blake* (Macmillan, 2nd ed. 1880), vol. I, ch. ii.
