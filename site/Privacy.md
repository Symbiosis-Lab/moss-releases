moss is a local‑first publishing tool made by **Symbiosis Lab**. The short version: **your writing stays on your computer**, we collect as little as we can, and there are **no ads and no usage tracking**.

This is an early policy — moss is in active development — and we'll expand it as the product grows. If anything here is unclear, [get in touch](#contact).

## Your content stays yours

Your folder of markdown files and everything in it lives **on your device**. It only leaves your computer when *you* choose to publish — and then only the finished website is hosted on our server and shown publicly, exactly as you intended.

## Your account

To publish or subscribe, we use your **email address** and your device's **public key** (a cryptographic ID — there's no password). We store these on our own server to know which sites are yours.

## Payments

Payments (site subscriptions and domain purchases) are handled by **Stripe**, a payment processor in the US. **Stripe holds your card details — we never see your card number.** We keep your Stripe customer ID and your email to manage your subscription; Stripe emails your receipt.

## Custom domains

If you **register a domain** through moss, the domain registrar (**OpenSRS / Tucows**, in Canada) requires ICANN contact details — your name, address, phone, and country. We turn on **WHOIS privacy** so those details aren't published publicly, and **we don't store them ourselves** (we keep only the domain name and your email). If you **connect a domain you already own**, we collect nothing extra.

## Newsletters (if you run one)

If your site has a newsletter, the **email addresses your readers sign up with** are stored for your site on our server, and newsletters are delivered through **Resend** (an email service in the US). We use confirmed opt‑in and **don't store readers' IP addresses**. As the site owner, your readers' data is your responsibility — we hold it on your behalf so you can email them.

## Comments (if you enable them)

When someone comments on your published site, their **name** (and optional email and website), their comment, and basic technical details — their **IP address and browser** — are stored in our **own self‑hosted comment system** (not a third party), as comment systems normally do. If reply notifications are enabled, those go out through Resend.

## Website analytics

For published sites we keep **basic, aggregate traffic stats** — country, browser, OS, and which pages were viewed. There are **no cookies, no cross‑site tracking, and no IP addresses** in these stats. (Our server's raw access logs may include visitor IPs for security and operations; they aren't used to track anyone.)

## Crash reports

If moss hits an error, it can send a **crash report** to our own server so we can fix bugs — this is the only thing moss reports automatically, and it does **not track how you use the app**. We strip personal data: **your IP isn't stored** (we mask it before it reaches our error tracker) and file paths are scrubbed. You can turn this off any time in **moss → Settings → Crash reporting**, and we delete crash reports after **90 days**.

## Support logs

If you use **"Send Logs"** to report a problem, moss uploads your recent app log (scrubbed of personal data) and any note you add — but only when *you* start it. We delete these after **30 days**.

## Where your data is processed

Our server is in the **United States**, and some of the services we rely on (Stripe, Resend, Cloudflare) are US‑based; our domain registrar (OpenSRS / Tucows) is in **Canada**. If you're in the EU or UK, your data may be processed outside your country.

## How long we keep things

Crash reports: 90 days. Support logs: 30 days. Your account, payment, and subscriber records are kept while your account or site is active. Want your data removed? Just ask.

## Your choices and rights

You can ask us to **see, correct, or delete** your data, and you can **object to crash reporting** at any time using the in‑app toggle. We rely on a **legitimate interest** in keeping moss reliable for crash reporting (not consent), and on our agreement with you for your account and payments.

moss is early, and this policy will grow with it. We'll update this page when our practices change.
